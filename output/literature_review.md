# Cocoa Claim-Support Literature Review

This review is now aligned to the manuscript claim-by-claim rather than to a broad disaster-resilience corpus. The retained papers were selected because they support one of the manuscript's identifiable concepts: benchmark transmission into producer-linked cocoa prices, uneven exposure within the cocoa value chain, or environmental stress as contextual vulnerability. Coffee analogies were removed wherever a cocoa-specific article could support the same idea more directly.

The first concept is that cocoa price volatility has resilience implications rather than being only a market outcome. Cocoa studies show that producer concerns often include price volatility, dependence on few buyers, weak farmer organizations, and exposure to climate stress, which means that livelihood vulnerability cannot be understood from prices alone or from agronomy alone \citep{Mithofer2017,AkrofiAtitianti2018}. Complementary agroforestry work argues that price volatility and climate change jointly reduce smallholders' ability to adapt when ecological buffering is weakened \citep{Vaast2014}. This supports the manuscript's opening claim that vulnerability is produced through the interaction between market instability and limited adaptive capacity.

The second concept is that producer exposure depends on transmission from wider markets. Cocoa market studies show that reforms and integration can increase farmers' exposure to world cocoa prices without necessarily increasing the share of value captured by farmers \citep{TsowouGayi2019}. Exchange-rate volatility is also relevant to cocoa pricing because it affects cocoa futures markets and therefore belongs to the broader macro-financial transmission environment \citep{Jumah2001}. These papers support the manuscript's decision to model world cocoa prices and the exchange rate as distinct transmission channels, while treating oil as an additional macro control rather than as a separate theoretical claim.

The third concept is that environmental stress matters, but not in the same way as benchmark transmission. Cocoa-specific climate studies show that vulnerability depends on climatic suitability, physiological sensitivity, management responses, and the limits to adaptation \citep{Schroth2016,Laderach2013,Lahive2019}. Farm- and system-level cocoa studies similarly show that resilience is shaped by agroecological design and producer practices, with agroforestry often improving buffering capacity, diversified incomes, and adaptation potential under climate stress \citep{Jacobi2013,Niether2020,Vaast2014}. These papers justify including localized weather indicators in the manuscript, but they do not justify replacing benchmark transmission with weather as the dominant short-run mechanism.

This evidence implies a tighter literature review logic for the paper. The manuscript should not claim to measure resilience in the abstract. It should claim to identify one mechanism of resilience-relevant exposure in cocoa systems: the transmission of external market shocks into producer-linked prices under macroeconomic conditions, interpreted alongside localized environmental stress. That framing is both better supported by the cocoa literature and more consistent with what the current methods can actually identify.

|---|---|---|
| Cocoa price volatility is a resilience issue, not only a market issue. | `Mithofer2017`, `AkrofiAtitianti2018`, `Vaast2014` | These papers connect cocoa producer livelihoods, weak market positions, climate stress, and adaptive capacity. |
| Vulnerability reflects transmitted shocks plus limited capacity to absorb them. | `TsowouGayi2019`, `Mithofer2017`, `Vaast2014` | These papers link world-market exposure, weak producer leverage, and reduced ability to adapt. |
| World cocoa prices and exchange-rate conditions are distinct transmission channels. | `TsowouGayi2019`, `Jumah2001` | These studies support modelling benchmark and currency conditions separately. |
| Downstream adjustment may differ from producer-linked adjustment. | `TsowouGayi2019`, `Mithofer2017` | These papers support the idea of uneven value-chain exposure, which the manuscript tests empirically with a downstream price index. |
| Weather should be treated as contextual vulnerability, not as the main short-run price mechanism. | `Schroth2016`, `Lahive2019`, `Jacobi2013`, `Niether2020` | These papers justify environmental stress as important for cocoa vulnerability while remaining distinct from price-transmission analysis. |
