# Gap-Aligned Disaster Resilience Review

This review has been narrowed to the studies that directly support the manuscript's contribution. The relevant gap is not whether disasters, climate stress, or governance matter in general. It is whether a resilience-oriented paper can identify how global cocoa shocks are transmitted into producer-linked prices, whether the supply chain distributes that adjustment unevenly, and whether weather should be incorporated as contextual vulnerability rather than as the dominant short-run price driver.

The retained transmission literature shows that producer exposure to world cocoa markets can intensify when integration strengthens the link between benchmark and domestic prices, but it also shows that exchange-rate conditions and other macroeconomic frictions shape how those shocks appear locally. This is why the manuscript models world cocoa prices, the COP/USD rate, and oil-price conditions together rather than reducing them to a single external volatility measure. The supply-chain literature adds that adaptive capacity is mediated by firms, cooperatives, and territorial governance, which implies that downstream prices may absorb or delay shocks differently from producer-linked prices. That insight directly supports the manuscript's comparison between Colombian cocoa prices and the European chocolate index.

The retained climate-resilience literature justifies the weather block, but in a narrower way than many broad resilience reviews imply. Climate-risk mapping and agroecological studies in coffee and cocoa systems show that vulnerability depends on the overlap of hazards, sensitivity, management systems, and adaptive capacity. They support the use of localized weather indicators to interpret periods of heightened stress. They do not, however, justify replacing benchmark price transmission with weather as the core explanation of short-run price changes. That distinction is essential to the manuscript's methodology.

The resulting synthesis supports one clear argument: resilience in the cocoa supply chain has to be analysed as the interaction of transmitted market shocks, macroeconomic conditions, uneven adjustment across chain positions, and local environmental stress. The manuscript fills that gap by keeping those channels distinct within one empirical design.
