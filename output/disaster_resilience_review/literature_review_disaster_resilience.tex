\section{Literature Review}

The literature relevant to this manuscript is narrower than the broader disaster-resilience field. For the present study, the critical gap is not simply whether disasters, climate stress, or governance matter for rural livelihoods. The more specific unresolved issue is how global cocoa shocks are transmitted into producer-linked prices, whether that adjustment is distributed unevenly across the supply chain, and how local weather stress should be incorporated without being overstated as the dominant short-run price mechanism.

Existing commodity studies provide only part of that answer. Evidence from cocoa-market integration shows that closer exposure to world markets can increase producer vulnerability, while exchange-rate movements and other macroeconomic conditions shape how global shocks are reflected in domestic prices \citep{TsowouGayi2019,Poku2017}. These studies justify the manuscript's decision to treat world cocoa prices, the exchange rate, and oil-price conditions as distinct components of the transmission environment rather than as interchangeable proxies for external volatility. What they do not provide is a resilience-oriented design that follows those shocks through both producer-linked and downstream consumer prices in one system.

The value-chain adaptation literature identifies a second missing piece. Research on coffee systems shows that adaptive capacity depends not only on farm-level choices, but also on how cooperatives, companies, and territorial institutions shape access to information, assets, and strategic options \citep{Morales2022,Verburg2019}. This implies that adjustment may be distributed unevenly across the chain, with some actors more buffered than others. The present manuscript takes that implication seriously by comparing Colombian producer-linked prices with a European downstream chocolate price index rather than treating downstream prices as background context.

Climate and agroecological studies identify the third component of the gap. Climate-risk mapping shows that vulnerability depends on the overlap of hazards, exposure, and adaptive capacity across producing regions \citep{Koh2020,Laderach2016}. Cocoa-specific work likewise shows that resilience is shaped by farm management, producer perceptions, and ecological design, with agroforestry often improving buffering capacity under heat and moisture stress \citep{Jacobi2013,Niether2020}. These studies strongly support the inclusion of localized weather indicators in the manuscript. At the same time, they do not imply that weather should replace benchmark price transmission as the core short-run mechanism. Their main value for the present paper is to justify weather as contextual vulnerability information.

The manuscript therefore fills a specific gap left open across these strands. It combines international cocoa benchmarks, Colombian producer-linked prices, a European downstream chocolate index, exchange-rate and oil controls, and localized weather indicators in one transparent empirical framework. The analytical gain comes from keeping those layers distinct: prices and macro controls identify transmission, downstream prices reveal the distribution of adjustment along the supply chain, and weather clarifies the local stress context in which transmitted shocks are experienced.
